Transient confirmation, and now it says WHICH kind. Tone = Success · Warning · Info, each with its own tint, its own glyph and neutral text. Measured before it was drawn: the app was already sending three different kinds of message down one channel with one appearance. «Ingreso registrado» and «Ingresa descripción y monto» looked identical — a confirmation and a validation error that blocks your save, in the same grey pill. The text is fg/default, NOT the tone. fg/success on bg/success-subtle measures 3.32 in Light, under the 4.5:1 that body text needs; fg/default on the same tint measures 15.74. So the tone lives in the surface and the glyph, where 3:1 applies, and the words stay readable. Warning uses fg/warning-strong, a rung minted for this: fg/warning on its own tint measures 2.86, under even the 3:1 for a graphic. It was the one tone whose icon could not be seen on its own surface. The glyph is not decoration — it is what keeps the meaning off colour alone (1.4.1): circle-check, triangle-alert, info. Three shapes, three meanings, legible in greyscale. THERE IS NO DANGER TONE. The app emits no true error through this channel — every red-looking message measured is a validation the user can fix, which is Warning. The library also has no circle-x. When a real error appears, the glyph and the tone come together. Still no action and no dismiss: it lasts 2200ms and leaves. If the user has to decide something, it is not a toast — it is a SystemMessage.
