The year’s gross, split into what it turned into: Obligaciones · Provisiones · Gastos · Neto libre. Four segments of chart/categorical/1–4 — the same four as TrendChart and DistribucionCard, so the three say the same thing with the same colours. 220 across with a 30 ring. The track underneath is bg/surface/subtle, so the ring still reads as a whole when one segment is a sliver. The centre is the readout, which is why this donut needs neither legend nor tooltip: by default it holds the gross, and selecting a segment moves that segment’s amount into the centre in its own colour while the others drop to 25%. Dimming the rest rather than lighting the one keeps the selected colour true — the colour is what names the segment. Each segment is a control with a name, a role and a key. Without that the amounts it reveals are mouse-only: unreachable by keyboard and unannounced to a screen reader. It appears only when there is more than one segment with an amount. A donut of one colour is a circle. The centre figure is Amount/Hero, 28, and it is ABBREVIATED: up to two decimals and a lowercase unit — «$54,57 m» for millions, «$1,20 k» for thousands. Alfredo's call, 2026-09-12. A year's gross written in full is eleven digits inside a 160 circle; either it shrinks until it stops being the headline, or it wraps. The decimal separator is the comma, es-CO, like every other figure in the app. The abbreviation stops at the centre: the axis labels stay «$12M», uppercase and without decimals, because an axis is a scale you read against and this is the figure you read.
